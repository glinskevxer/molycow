# glossary

The words this pharmacy uses, and what they mean here.

**assay** — The reading a dose calibrator gives for what is actually in a syringe or a vial.

**authorized user** — A physician named on a customer's licence for particular nuclides. Nothing is dispensed except against one.

**batch** — One cold kit after the eluate has gone in, with its own activity, volume and expiry. Also called a bulk vial.

**breakthrough** — Molybdenum that came off the column with the technetium. The limit is a ratio at the moment of injection, and the ratio climbs all day.

**calibration time** — The moment a dose is meant to read the ordered activity, which is when the department will inject it.

**cold kit** — A vial of freeze dried chemistry with no activity in it, which becomes a radiopharmaceutical when eluate is added.

**constancy** — The daily check that a dose calibrator still reads a long lived sealed source the same as it did yesterday.

**decay in storage** — Holding waste for ten half lives of the longest lived thing in it, then surveying it at background before it is disposed of.

**eluate** — What comes off the column: sodium pertechnetate in saline, with a twelve hour shelf life from the moment it was drawn.

**elution** — One washing out of a generator, and the vial of eluate it produced. Also called a milking.

**generator** — A sealed column of molybdenum-99 that is washed out to collect the technetium-99m that has grown in from it. Also called a cow.

**ingrowth** — The technetium building back up in the column between elutions. It peaks just short of a day after a full strip.

**possession limit** — The most of one nuclide the licence allows on the premises at any one moment, counted by decaying everything to the same moment.

**radiochemical purity** — The share of the activity in a batch that is bound to the right chemistry, read off two chromatography strips. Also called rcp.

**transport index** — The dose rate a metre from a package, in millirem an hour, rounded up to a tenth. It decides the label and the van's load. Also called ti.

**unit dose** — One syringe drawn for one patient, calibrated for one moment.

**wipe** — A swab of a surface, counted in a well counter and converted to disintegrations a minute to compare against the contamination limit. Also called a smear.
